Rules
  • Hotter, sweatier → more water first, then more salt. Same sugar. Never all three at once.
  • Longer → more sugar, tied to the run's length, not to how much you sweat.
  • Concentration: at or under 6% carbs in the heat, 8% in aircon. Above that it sits in your stomach. Need more than the drink can carry? Gels chased with water.
  • Sodium: 450–850 mg per litre in heat, upper half if you're a salty sweater (white streaks on cap or shirt). 1 g table salt ≈ 390 mg sodium. Plain water when there's no sugar in it — salty water alone is unpleasant.
  • Fluid: about three quarters of your sweat rate, cap ~1 L/h. Losing up to 2% body weight in a run is fine.
  • Treadmill without a fan is a hot run: no airflow, sweat doesn't evaporate.
  • Pre-threshold snack: chocolate bars carry fat that slows gastric emptying. Fine 1–2 h before; in the last 15 min it's LOT 100 gummies — 5 Fruity = 25.5 g carbs, zero fat, no weighing (the "Before threshold" preset). Sour+ is ~27% denser and a touch cheaper per serving — 6 pieces ≈ 27 g (about 4.8 g per piece); sodium in both is negligible.
  • Table sugar is half glucose, half fructose — the two-transporter mix. Plenty up to 60 g/h; maltodextrin only matters for concentrated race gels.
  • Prep: sugar + salt dry in the bottle the night before; cordial, water, shake in the morning.
Cordials — flavour only, the sugar is the fuel
The mixer assumes a typical 55 g carbs per 100 mL of cordial; the two brands are close enough that it doesn't change the recipe. N8 Refuel: 25 g carbs, 40 mg sodium, ~RM8.50 per gel.
Sources: Jeukendrup 2014, A step towards personalized sports nutrition: carbohydrate intake during exercise (Sports Med) · Sawka et al. 2007, ACSM position stand Exercise and fluid replacement · Thomas, Erdman & Burke 2016, ACSM/AND/DC joint position Nutrition and athletic performance · Carter et al. 2004, The effect of carbohydrate mouth rinse on 1-h cycle time trial performance (MSSE).
